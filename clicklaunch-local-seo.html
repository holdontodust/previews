What is local SEO?

It's how a business gets found by people nearby who are ready to hire. When someone searches “plumber near me” or opens Google Maps, local SEO is what decides which businesses show up. It covers your Google Business Profile, your reviews, your website clearly stating your services and service areas, and your business information being consistent everywhere it appears online. Regular SEO is about the whole internet — local SEO is about your own backyard, where your customers actually are.

Why is my business not showing up on Google?

Usually one of a few fixable reasons. Your Google Business Profile may not be claimed or verified yet. Your business info — name, phone, hours, services — may be missing, wrong, or inconsistent across the web. Your website may not clearly say what you do and where you do it. Or competitors near you simply have stronger profiles and more reviews, so Google shows them first. And sometimes it's just newness: a new business or website can take a while to get picked up. The fix starts with finding which of these it is — that's the first thing we check. Full breakdown: why is my business not showing up on Google?

How long does local SEO take to work?

SEO is a build, not a switch. Most businesses start seeing movement in the first few months — showing up for more searches, more calls from Google — with stronger results building over three to six months and beyond. Anyone who promises you page one by next week isn't being straight with you; Google doesn't run on anyone's schedule. What you get from us is steady work every month and a report showing exactly what changed.

What is the Google map pack?

It's the block of three businesses Google shows on a map at the top of local search results, right under the ads. For most local searches it's the most clicked spot on the page — it's where “near me” customers pick who to call. Local SEO is largely about earning one of those three spots for the searches that matter to your business.

How do reviews affect local SEO?

Reviews are one of the strongest signals Google uses to rank local businesses, and they're the first thing customers check. How many you have, how recent they are, and whether you respond all count. Businesses that ask every happy customer, every time, pull away from competitors who wait for reviews to happen on their own. Our review engine makes asking automatic, so they build steadily instead of in bursts.

Can I do local SEO myself?

The basics, yes — and you should. Claim and completely fill out your Google Business Profile, keep your hours and info accurate, ask happy customers for reviews, and make sure your website clearly lists your services and the areas you serve. Where it gets hard is the ongoing part: knowing which changes actually move the needle, doing them every month, and reading the data to adjust. Most owners would rather spend those hours on the work they're paid for. That's the part we take off your plate.

Do I need local SEO if most of my work comes from referrals?

Referrals are gold, but even referred customers usually Google you before they call — and if they find a thin profile and no reviews, some quietly go with the competitor who looked established. Local SEO also covers the customers who don't know anyone to ask: they're searching right now, ready to hire. If you're booked solid and turning work away, you may not need more. If you'd take more of the right jobs, this is where they are.

How do I rank in the Google map pack?

Three things decide it most: a complete, verified Google Business Profile with the right categories; a steady flow of real reviews; and a website that clearly names your services and service areas, with business info that matches your profile everywhere online. Proximity matters too — Google shows businesses near the searcher — so you earn the spot area by area, not everywhere at once. There is no submission or fee that buys a place; it is earned by being the most complete, best-reviewed option nearby. That is exactly the build our local SEO service runs every month.
