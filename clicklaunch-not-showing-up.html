Why is my business not showing up on Google?

It's the most common question local businesses ask, and the answer is almost never “Google has something against you.” It's one of a handful of fixable reasons. Here they are, how to tell which one is yours, and what fixes each.

The usual reasons

Work down the list. Most invisible businesses have two or three of these stacked together.

1. Your Google Business Profile isn't claimed or verified

Maps and local results run on this profile. If you never claimed it, or verification never finished, Google has nothing it's willing to show.

The fix: claim the profile and complete verification. Nothing else on this list matters until this is done.

2. Your profile is claimed but half empty

No services listed, no hours, no photos, wrong category. Google ranks complete profiles over skeletons, and customers skip them too.

The fix: fill in every field — categories, each service you offer, hours, service area, real photos of your work.

3. Your business info doesn't match across the web

Old phone number on one site, a different name spelling on another, an address you moved from two years ago. Mismatches make Google unsure which business is real, so it shows the ones it's sure about.

The fix: one correct name, phone, and address, made consistent everywhere your business appears.

4. Your website doesn't say what you do or where

A homepage that says “welcome to our website” and little else gives Google nothing to match against searches. If your services and service areas aren't written on the site in plain words, you can't show up for them.

The fix: real pages that name each service and the areas you serve, answering the questions customers actually ask.

5. Your competitors have stronger reviews

Same town, same trade: the business with 140 recent reviews beats the one with 9 from 2022. Reviews are a ranking signal and the first thing customers read.

The fix: ask every happy customer, every time, and make asking automatic so reviews build steadily.

6. You're new

A new business, a new profile, or a new website takes time to get picked up and trusted. This one is real, and it's the only one that fixes itself.

The fix: patience, plus doing reasons 1 through 5 right so the clock works for you instead of against you.

7. You are showing up — just not where customers look

Page two of Google and below the map three-pack might as well be invisible. Most clicks go to the map block and the first few results.

The fix: this is the long game of local SEO: profile strength, reviews, and a website built to be found. See local SEO services for how that build works.

Check yourself in two minutes

  1. Open a private or incognito browser window (so your own history doesn't fool you) and search your main service plus your town, the way a customer would.
  2. Check the map block first. Are you in it? Anywhere on the map list?
  3. Now search your exact business name. If you don't show up even for your own name, it's a profile problem — reasons 1 and 2. If your name works but the service search doesn't, it's a visibility problem — reasons 3 through 7.

Why is my business not showing up on Google Maps?

Maps results come almost entirely from your Google Business Profile. If yours is unclaimed, unverified, or barely filled in, Maps has nothing to show. The other common cause: your category or service area is wrong, so Google doesn't connect you to the searches happening around you. It's fixable — claim the profile, verify it, fill in every service, and keep it current.

How long until a new business shows up on Google?

A claimed and verified profile can appear within days. Building real visibility — showing up for service searches, not just your own name — takes longer: weeks to appear consistently, and a few months of steady work to compete with established businesses. Newness is a real factor, but it's the one that fixes itself while you work on the others.

My competitors show up on Google but I don't. Why?

Almost never luck. Compare the basics side by side: their profile is usually complete where yours has gaps, they have more reviews and fresher ones, and their website clearly states their services and areas while yours is thin or missing. Google shows the businesses it can understand and trust. Closing those gaps is most of the work.

Does my website affect my Google Maps ranking?

Yes. Google cross-checks your profile against your website. A site that clearly lists your services and service areas, with the same name, phone, and details as your profile, strengthens both. A missing site, or one whose info doesn't match your profile, weakens the whole picture.

Will running Google ads fix my visibility?

Ads buy you the top slots while you pay, and they stop the moment you stop. They don't fix your profile, your reviews, or your website, and they don't move your organic or map rankings. Ads can make sense as a bridge, but the underlying visibility problem still has to be fixed — that's the part that keeps working after the spending stops.

What should I fix first if my business is not showing up on Google?

Your Google Business Profile, in this order: claim it, verify it, then complete every field — categories, services, hours, photos, service area. Then make sure your name, phone, and details match everywhere they appear online. Then your website: one clear statement of what you do and where, per service. Reviews come next and never really stop. If that list sounds like a second job, it's exactly the list we work through for clients.
